Dealing With Roof Leak Water Damage in Harlingen

A roof leak rarely starts where the stain appears. A loose shingle, a failed flashing joint or an aging seal around a skylight lets rain in, and it travels across the roof deck and framing until it falls into the insulation. Often the attic has been damp for some time before any brown mark reaches the room below. We find the full wet area and dry it out.

To be clear, roof repair is not what we do, water damage is. We work on the interior, which means removing wet insulation, drying drywall and framing, dealing with mold and rebuilding the ceiling. When the roof is still letting water through, a temporary tarp is something we can install, and we will coordinate with your roofer so the repair comes before the ceiling is closed.

Before We Arrive

  1. Cut Power to Wet FixturesWater coming through a ceiling fan, a light or close to an outlet means that circuit should be turned off at the panel, and the fixture should not be touched.
  2. Catch the DripsPut a pan or bucket beneath each drip, and shift rugs, electronics and furniture clear of the wet area. Protect the floor with plastic sheeting or old towels.
  3. Drain a Bulging CeilingIf the ceiling is holding a pocket of water, stand to one side and poke a small hole at the lowest point so it drains into a bucket.
  4. Leave the Roof AloneA wet roof and an unlit attic are both places to stay out of. Note from inside where the water is coming in and leave the rest to professionals.
  5. Document What You SeeTake photos and video of the ceiling, the attic and any damaged belongings before you move or throw anything away, then call us to start drying.

Our Roof Leak Water Damage Process

Trace the Leak Path

We inspect the attic, ceilings and wall cavities with moisture meters and thermal imaging to map everywhere the rain traveled, including areas that still look dry from the room below.

When rain can still get in, a temporary tarp from us or a call to your roofer comes first. Anything sitting beneath the damage, including the flooring, gets protected too.

Why It Happens

Why This Happens

  • Nails that have backed out of the decking and left small holes through the shingles.
  • Gutters packed with leaves, which push water back beneath the first row of shingles.
  • Aging, cracked seals around a skylight frame that no longer keep water out.
  • Holes and fractures left in the roof after a branch or hailstones hit it.
  • Wind-torn, curling or split shingles that leave the underlayment open to rain.
If It Is Left

Where the Damage Spreads

Lights and Wiring

Leak water heads for openings in the ceiling, such as recessed lights, fan boxes and junction boxes. Moisture in a fixture can cause a short and later corrosion, which is why it needs inspection before use.

Roof Framing

Repeated wetting makes decking, trusses and rafters turn dark, swell up and go soft. Decay here means less strength in what carries the roof.

Attic Insulation

Wet insulation mats down, stops insulating and holds water against the ceiling drywall for days. In some cases fiberglass batts dry out and stay. Removal is the usual answer for wet cellulose.

Mold in the Attic

Damp framing, paper-faced drywall, warmth and darkness give mold what it needs. Growth can begin within 24 to 48 hours and spread across the underside of the decking.

Coverage

Insurance and Roof Leak Water Damage

Homeowners policies often cover interior water damage when a roof leak is sudden and accidental, such as wind tearing off shingles or a limb puncturing the roof. Leaks blamed on age, wear or skipped maintenance are usually denied, and the roof repair itself may be handled differently from the interior. Our team gives your adjuster photos and moisture readings, yet what is covered comes down to your policy, so check it or talk to your agent.
