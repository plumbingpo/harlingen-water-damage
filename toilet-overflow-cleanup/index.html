Dealing With Toilet Overflow Cleanup in Harlingen

A toilet overflow looks like a mop job, but the water does not stay on the tile. The gap at the drain pipe, the wall and the space under the baseboard all take water in. Most important is what the water was carrying. Clean water is what comes out of the supply line or the tank. Water that came up through the bowl is not, and it has to be handled as contaminated.

In Harlingen, we begin each overflow job by determining the category of water, which decides how the rest is done. If it is clean, we extract it and dry the area. Gray or black water means protective gear, removal of soaked porous materials like carpet pad, and disinfection of every surface it touched. Then we check the subfloor, the wall bases and the room underneath with moisture meters so nothing is left wet.

Right Now

What to Do Right Now

  1. Shut Off the SupplyTurn the valve behind the toilet clockwise until it stops. A stuck valve can be worked around by taking off the tank lid and lifting the float, which stops the fill.
  2. Stop FlushingHold off on flushing, and keep the washer, showers and sinks off too. If the drain line is blocked, any water you send down can come back up.
  3. Avoid Dirty WaterBowl water, or any water with waste in it, means kids and pets stay out of the room. Should you have to cross it, wear gloves and waterproof footwear.
  4. Look at the Ceiling UnderneathIf the toilet is upstairs, go check the ceiling directly below it. A ceiling that sags or drips means turning off that room's power and moving your things clear.
  5. Contain Clean WaterOld towels can stop clean water before it gets to a hallway or carpet. Anything contaminated is a job for people with proper gear and disinfecting products.
Our Process

How We Handle Toilet Overflow Cleanup

First we determine if the source was the bowl and drain or the supply side. Proper containment and protection are put in place before cleanup starts.

2

Our crew removes the standing water, takes out any baseboard, drywall or carpet pad that is contaminated, and pulls the toilet if water got below its base and the flange.

The bathroom, plus any room below that got wet, is dried with dehumidifiers and air movers. Our crew keeps metering the subfloor and the ceiling until both read normal again.

Coverage

What About Insurance?

Many homeowners policies cover a toilet overflow when it is sudden and accidental, such as a failed fill valve or an unexpected clog. Sewer or drain backup is often excluded without a backup endorsement on the policy, and an overflow from a blocked main line can fall under that exclusion. Damage from a toilet that leaked at the base for months is usually not covered. To learn which of these applies in your case, check the policy or phone your agent.

Common Causes

What Causes It

  • Waste water with nowhere to go because the septic tank is full or the drain field is saturated.
  • A main sewer line blockage that backs waste up through the lowest toilet.
  • Supply lines that give out or tanks that crack, releasing clean water across the floor.
  • A clog in the trap or drain line that sends bowl water over the rim.
  • A worn wax ring that lets water out at the base each time the toilet is flushed.
If It Is Left

What the Water Damages

Soaked Subfloor

Through the grout and around the toilet base, water works its way below tile or vinyl flooring. Plywood below swells and delaminates, which can release the flooring glued to it.

Bacteria in Building Materials

Carpet, pad, drywall and baseboards all pick up bacteria from bowl or drain water. Porous materials cannot be reliably disinfected and usually have to be removed.

Wet Vanity and Wall Bases

The bottom of the drywall and the toe kick of the vanity take in water and pull it upward. The wall can be wet several inches above the floor while the paint still looks fine.

Ceiling Below

From a second floor bathroom, water follows the drain pipe down and pools on the ceiling drywall beneath, leaving stains, sagging and sometimes a collapse.
