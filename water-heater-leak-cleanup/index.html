Water Heater Leak Cleanup Help in Harlingen

A water heater holds 40 to 80 gallons, but that is not the limit of the leak. Because the supply line feeds the tank, a rusted out bottom drains and refills at the same pace. Nothing stops the flow except shutting the cold water valve. How bad it gets depends on placement, whether that is the garage, a closet in the hall or an attic sitting above finished rooms, which is the worst.

In Harlingen, we start with water extraction and then inspect everything around the heater. Usually the tank platform and the lower part of the closet walls have taken on the most water. Garage leaks wet whatever is stored on the floor, along with the wall the garage shares with the house. Attic leaks travel downward through the insulation and the ceilings below. We measure each area and dry it with air movers and dehumidifiers.

Do This First

Before We Arrive

  1. Turn Off the Heat SourceFor an electric heater, switch off its breaker. For gas, turn the control knob to off. Never leave a heating element or burner running on an empty tank.
  2. Close the Cold SupplyThe cold line over the tank has a valve, and it closes with a clockwise turn. A stuck or leaking valve means going to the house main and shutting the water off there.
  3. Watch for Hot WaterWhat comes out of the tank can cause scalds. Keep children and pets away, and do not wade through pooled water until it has had time to cool.
  4. Empty the Tank OutsideIf you can reach the drain valve, attach a garden hose and run it outside or to a floor drain so the remaining water does not empty onto the floor.
  5. Move Stored ItemsAnything absorbent, including boxes and rugs, should come up from the wet area. Cardboard and fabric soak up water quickly and hold it against walls and concrete.
Insurance

What About Insurance?

When a tank fails suddenly, the water damage to floors, walls and belongings is often covered as sudden and accidental. Replacing the heater is usually not included, because a tank wearing out from age counts as maintenance. Rust trails, old staining or other evidence that the leak was long-term can lead an adjuster to reduce or deny the claim, since gradual leaks usually fall outside coverage. Read your policy, take photos of the damage and the heater, and do not dispose of the old unit before the claim is settled.

Cost

What Affects the Cost

  • Placement of the unit, as leaks from an attic or an inside closet run higher than those in a garage.
  • Whether flooring in adjoining rooms was reached and what type it is.
  • Amount of contents affected, since wet belongings have to be relocated, dried or discarded.
  • How much insulation, ceiling or wall drywall needs to be taken out.

Why It Happens

How It Usually Starts

  • An overflow pan that cannot drain, because its line is clogged or the pan itself has rusted.
  • Fittings left slightly loose by recent repair or installation work.
  • Sediment and corrosion that eat through the bottom of an aging steel tank.
  • Water discharged when the pressure and temperature relief valve fails or opens.
  • Anode rods that are used up, leaving the steel inside the tank open to rust.

What Happens if You Wait

Items Stored Near the Heater

Whatever is kept by a closet or garage heater, from keepsakes to furniture and boxes, is the first thing to get wet. Cardboard falls apart, and wet contents can mold if they are not dried or discarded quickly.

Stand and Subfloor Damage

The wooden stand or subfloor under the tank takes the most water. Rot can weaken it, a real concern once a full new tank sits on top.

Closet Walls

Water wicks up the drywall around an interior heater closet and into the rooms on the other side. Baseboards swell and the lower wall stays wet inside.

Garage Wall Base

As water runs across the garage slab, it wets the drywall and bottom plate of the wall the garage shares with the house and continues into flooring on the other side.

How We Fix It

How We Handle Water Heater Leak Cleanup

1

Remove Standing Water

Standing water is taken up from the garage, closet or floor. With an attic unit we also clear the ceiling cavity and overflow pan, which keeps nearby materials from soaking up more.

2

Check Around the Heater

To learn the full reach of the water, our crew meters and scans the platform, the bottoms of the walls, neighboring rooms and the ceiling below if there is one.

3

Take Out What Will Not Dry

We take out baseboards that are damaged, drywall that is crumbling and insulation that is soaked. In attics we clear wet insulation so the ceiling joists and drywall below can be dried.

4

Drying and Moisture Readings

Drying usually takes 3 to 5 days, and dehumidifiers and air movers run the whole time. Moisture readings are recorded, and you hear from us once the space can take the new heater.
